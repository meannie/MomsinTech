# Career output guide

Use only the section matching the requested deliverable.

## Performance review or promotion case

Group receipts around 2–4 themes rather than listing everything chronologically. Connect contribution, evidence, scope, and organizational value. Include a short evidence appendix mapping claims to receipt IDs. Flag gaps or uncertain attribution instead of smoothing them over.

## Resume

Select receipts relevant to the target role. Write concise, active bullets that state contribution and outcome, adding scale when it clarifies significance. Do not force every bullet into a numeric formula. Preserve team context with language such as “led,” “partnered,” or “contributed.”

If a job description is supplied, prioritize alignment without copying its claims or keywords unnaturally.

## Interview

Build a flexible STAR outline:

- **Situation:** context and stakes
- **Task:** the user's responsibility
- **Action:** choices and specific contribution
- **Result:** observed outcome, learning, or risk avoided
- **Evidence:** one or two details the user can defend
- **Adaptation:** interview questions this story can answer

Do not invent dialogue, motivations, conflict, or a polished resolution absent from the receipt.

## LinkedIn

First create a public-safe version using the privacy guide. Lead with a concrete observation or result, give enough context to make it useful, and include an honest learning. Avoid fabricated vulnerability, humblebragging, and engagement bait. A closing question is optional.

## Vault review

Summarize themes, strongest supported outcomes, receipts awaiting follow-up, and overlooked forms of impact. Suggest no more than three high-value follow-ups at once.
