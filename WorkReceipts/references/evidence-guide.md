# Evidence guide

Ask only about dimensions that materially strengthen the receipt.

## Useful evidence types

- **Business:** revenue, cost, retention, conversion, pipeline, time to value
- **Customer:** satisfaction, feedback themes, adoption, accessibility, problems resolved
- **Delivery:** cycle time, throughput, launch quality, dependencies cleared
- **Technical:** reliability, latency, security, incidents, maintainability, scale
- **Leadership:** decisions enabled, alignment created, talent developed, conflict resolved
- **Operations:** hours saved, errors prevented, handoffs improved, continuity protected
- **Community:** participation, reach, belonging, access, volunteer enablement
- **Risk avoided:** prevented loss, compliance exposure, rework, outage, churn, or burnout
- **Learning:** validated or rejected a hypothesis, exposed a constraint, changed the roadmap

## Prompting

Prefer a specific question tied to the work:

- “What became easier or possible after you did this?”
- “Who felt the effect, and at roughly what scale?”
- “Is there a dashboard, message, decision, or piece of feedback that supports it?”
- “What part did you own, and what did the team own?”
- “Was the result observed, estimated, or still pending?”

Avoid “Do you have any metrics?” and long questionnaires.

## Evidence integrity

- Keep the user's approximation markers: `about`, `roughly`, or `estimated`.
- If attribution is uncertain, write “contributed to” rather than “caused.”
- If a result belongs to a team, state the user's role inside that result.
- A pending result can still be a receipt. Capture the contribution and a concrete follow-up.
- Qualitative evidence is valid when it is specific: who said what, what decision changed, or what risk was reduced.
